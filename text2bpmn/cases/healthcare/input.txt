The process begins when the Patient develops new symptoms or experiences a deterioration of a pre-existing condition. Depending on the severity of the symptoms, the process branches into either an emergency or non-emergency pathway. For acute severe symptoms, the Patient or their Family contacts emergency services or presents directly to the Emergency Department. The Emergency Department evaluates the Patient to make a triage decision: they either admit the Patient to the Hospital for urgent care, or they discharge the Patient with treatment recommendations. If discharged, follow-up care is managed by the General Practitioner, or the Patient is referred to Outpatient Cardiology. For mild symptoms or known conditions, the Patient takes the non-emergency route by visiting the General Practitioner. The General Practitioner adjusts or initiates treatment and recommends a specialized evaluation. Alternatively, the Patient can choose a private route and consult directly with Outpatient Cardiology. During the specialized evaluation, Outpatient Cardiology establishes the final diagnosis, prescribes treatment, and sets up periodic follow-ups. However, upon assessing the complexity of the case, Outpatient Cardiology may decide the case is too complex for outpatient care and will recommend admitting the Patient to the Hospital. Once admitted to the Hospital, medical history must be gathered. The Patient provides this directly to the Hospital staff; however, in situations where the Patient is unable to communicate, the Family provides the medical history instead. Based on the final diagnosis, the Hospital administers the appropriate medical, interventional, or surgical treatment. Throughout the stay, the Hospital maintains communication with the Family to continuously inform them of the Patient’s progress. When treatment is complete, the Hospital prepares the discharge and gives the Patient a discharge summary containing treatment recommendations and a follow-up plan. The process concludes when the Patient submits this discharge summary to their General Practitioner.
